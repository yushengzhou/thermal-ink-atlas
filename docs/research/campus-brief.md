# Research brief: school campus playground (2026-11-27)

Bottom line: on clear summer days (Ta 30–36 °C) dry crumb-rubber artificial turf (AT) peaks ≈65–80 °C, 20–37 K hotter than natural grass (≈37–40 °C); at night AT returns to grass temperature (low admittance). Irrigating AT cools for minutes, not hours. Shade sails cut dark rubber by ≈40 K. No measured child-height vs adult-height Tmrt found, and the engine cannot resolve it at 5 m cells — do not quote one. Confidence high for turf/grass and burn thresholds, medium-low for track/acrylic (vendor or weak sources).

## Evidence
- Jim 2016 Applied Energy 173:520 / Jim 2017 LUP 157:561 (via Singh et al. 2024 systematic review, Int J Biometeorol, doi 10.1007/s00484-024-02679-5): AT 74.6 vs grass 36.9 °C; AT 73.8 vs 37.3 at Ta 34.4; air at 15 cm over AT 54.2 vs 36.0 at 150 cm; daily minimum AT 23.1–23.4 ≈ grass 23.1.
- Singh 2024 (23 studies): AT 9.4–33.7 K hotter than grass at surface, 0.5–1.2 K in air; Tmrt/WBGT inconsistent. Maxima: Twomey 2016 86.6; Petrass 2014b 80.1 vs 53.2; McNitt 2008 71.5 at Ta 30.6; Liu & Jim 2021 65.3 vs 40.4 at Ta 34.8. SBR 53.5 vs TPE 45.6 (lab); pad +1.8 K; fibre colour 1–2 K.
- BYU 2002 / Penn State (NY DOH fact sheet): AT avg 47 vs grass 26, asphalt 43 °C, max 93 °C; Penn State plots 49/54/63 at Ta 26–29; PSU: AT 20–30 K hotter than grass.
- Pfautsch et al. 2020 NSW school microclimate report: sunlit AT 51.7/56.1/59.2 (max 67.5) normal/hot/extreme; asphalt 44.8/51.1/54.4 (max 61.6); shaded AT 35 vs 61 sunlit; shade cooled black asphalt ≈25 K, black globe > 10 K.
- Vanos et al. 2016 LUP 146:29 (doi 10.1016/j.landurbplan.2015.10.007), Gilbert AZ noon Ta 41.6: rubber 87.2 sun / 42.2 tree / 46.7 sail; slide 71.7 → 43.9.
- PU track weak: Kinmen GLOBE report > Ta+15 K at noon; Aoki 2004 track 42.2 vs AT 67.0 (unverified). Acrylic courts vendor-only: 52 °C at Ta ≈30 (CoolTop; Laykold).
- Albedo AT 0.073 (0.05–0.15) Jim; 0.10–0.12 Loveday; 0.069 Wardenaar. Grass 0.16–0.33 / 0.20–0.22; Vanos 2017 grass 0.22–0.26, asphalt court 0.24.
- Irrigation: BYU 79 → 29 °C, back to 49 after 5 min, 73 after 20 min; Penn State 54–71 → 24, 32–49 within 30 min, stable 3 h (McNitt et al. 2008 Acta Hort 783:573).
- Vanos et al. 2017 Build Environ 126:119 (Lubbock): shade sail −168 W m⁻² absorbed by children; concrete sun/shade 40.0/29.2, 43.8/32.7, 44.4/34.1.
- Middel et al. 2021 BAMS: building shade > trees > lightweight shade; all shade types positive ΔTmrt after sunset (per-type numbers unverified).
- Children: core closer to hot ground (Vanos 2017); Vanos 2015 review; air at 0.3 m up to 1 K warmer than 1.5 m in weak wind (Paolini, ICUC12-249); indoor child projected-area factor differs ≤22 % → ≈ +1 K Tmrt (ÉTS).
- Contact burn thresholds, ISO 13732-1 via CENELEC Guide 29 Table A.1: metal 51/48/43 °C (1 min/10 min/8 h); stone/concrete 56/48/43; plastics/wood 60/48/43; use low end for children. Vanos press: 60 °C burns in 1 min, 74 in 5 s, 77 in 3 s (standard unnamed, unverified). CPSC burn-case counts via NJ DCA (unverified).

## Acceptance bands (Ta peak 35)
AT 65–80 °C at 14–15 h; AT − grass +20 to +40 K; grass 33–42; AT − grass ±2 K at 05:00; track Ta+15–30 and < AT (unverified); acrylic Ta+15–25 (vendor); rubber under sail Ta+0–8 and ≥25 K below sunlit; tree shade Ta+0–5; irrigation → within 5 K of Ta, 30 min later 30–90 % recovered, 3 h residual < 10 K; Tmrt sun − shade at noon 15–30 K (unverified).

## Disclose
Air fixed (no near-ground air gradient: +18 K at 15 cm over AT not modelled; child exposure understated); no height-resolved Tmrt → qualitative child warning; burn thresholds depend on material/contact time; irrigation lasts minutes; shade structures hold heat after sunset.
