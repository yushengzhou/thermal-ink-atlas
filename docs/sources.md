Not used by the grid model:

- `water`: Not used by the grid model: the pond is held at daily mean air - 1 K, +-1 K (research brief).
- `canopy`: Not used by the grid model: leaf temperature = air + 2.5 K x (K/900 W m-2) by day, air - 0.5 K at night (engine assumption; brief: air +0-3 K).
- `river`: Not used by the grid model: the river is held at daily mean air - 2 K, +-0.5 K (peak at 15:00), so it stays below the air all afternoon (research brief, block 4; offset and amplitude are assumptions).
- `pond`: Not used by the grid model: the pond is held at daily mean air - 0.5 K, +-2 K (peak at 16:00): cooler than the afternoon air, warmer than the air before dawn (research brief, block 4; numbers are assumptions, the warm night is cited).
- `youngCanopy`: Not used by the grid model: leaf temperature as for mature trees. Its thin crown lets through exp(-0.5 x 1.5) = 0.47 of the sun (see youngTreeTransmission).

Shown on the heat cards as a caveat:

- `grass`: Its 3 pm place below shaded paving holds only with evaporative fraction and albedo at the top of their cited ranges (see Sensitivity in docs/sources.md).
- `coolroof`: Its 3 pm place below shaded paving holds only with albedo at the top of its cited range (see Sensitivity in docs/sources.md).

- **[steadman1994]** Steadman (1994) Australian Meteorological Magazine 43, apparent temperature with radiation: AT = Ta + 0.348 e - 0.70 ws + 0.70 Q / (ws + 10) - 4.25 (as used by the Australian Bureau of Meteorology; coefficients from memory, not checked)
- **[noaa]** NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory, General Solar Position Calculations (solareqns.PDF)
- **[brutsaert]** Brutsaert (1975) Water Resources Research 11(5) 742-744, clear-sky emissivity
- **[meinel]** Meinel & Meinel (1976) Applied Solar Energy, clear-sky beam irradiance
- **[solweig]** Lindberg, Holmer & Thorsson (2008) Int. J. Biometeorol. 52, 697-713, SOLWEIG mean radiant temperature

## Equations

- Sun position [noaa]: fractional-year γ, equation of time, declination, true solar time, hour angle → zenith and azimuth.
- Surface energy balance, force-restore [deardorff]:
  dTs/dt = (2√π / (μ√τ))·G − (2π/τ)·(Ts − Td), τ = 86400 s, Td = previous-day mean of Ts.
  Thin layers (sheet-metal roofs) use dTs/dt = G / C with no restore term [brief].
  1-min steps; the design day repeats until max |ΔTd| < 0.05 K (at least 3 days; this scene converges after 7 days, final max |ΔTd| = 0.030 K).
  G = (1−α)·K↓ + ε·L↓ − εσTs⁴ − h_c·(Ts − Ta) − EF·max(Q*, 0). The brief writes EF·Q*; clipping at 0 is an *engine assumption* so latent heat never warms a surface at night.
- Shortwave: K↓ = sunlit·I_b·sinβ + SVF·D + K_wr (+ r·I_b·sinβ off glass, block 3), I_b = 1361·0.7^(AM^0.678) [meinel], D = 0.12·I_b·sinβ [brief]; tree shade transmits exp(−0.5·LAI) ≈ 0.17 (LAI 3.5 is an *engine assumption*).
- Longwave: L↓ = SVF·ε_sky·σTa⁴ + (1−SVF)·L_wall, L_wall = σT_wall⁴ for opaque walls (glass walls: block 3), ε_sky = 1.24·(e_a/Ta)^(1/7) [brutsaert], T_wall = Ta + 2 K + 6 K·(K↓/900) (*engine assumption*, peaks at 6.8 K + 2 K, ignores wall orientation).
- Convection: h_c = 5.7 + 3.8·u [oke], the same for roofs and ground.
- Water: Ts = daily mean Ta − 1 K + 1 K·sin(2π(t − 9 h)/24) [brief] (blocks 1–3; block 4's river and pond: see Block 4).
- Convection, block 4: h_c = 5.7 + 3.8·u_site·f_w per cell, f_w the wind factor (see Block 4); blocks 1–3 have f_w = 1.
- Tree leaves: air + 2.5 K·(K↓/900) by day, air − 0.5 K at night (*engine assumption*; brief: air +0–3 K).
- Air temperature [brief]: sinusoid with Ta,min at sunrise and Ta,max at 15:00, implemented as two half-cosines (rise and fall).
- Sky view factor: horizon scan, SVF = mean over 16 azimuths of cos²(horizon elevation); canyon check against cos(arctan(2H/W)).
- Shortwave reflected by opaque walls (all blocks, added in #8) [brief3]: K_wr = α_w·(1 − SVF_open)·c·K_wall with
  K_wall = f_lit·I_b·cosβ/π + 0.5·D + 0.5·α_g·G, α_w = 0.3 (*cited range*, unverified), f_lit = 0.5 (*engine assumption*),
  α_g the cell's own albedo, G = I_b·sinβ + D unshaded, SVF_open the building-only sky view factor and c = 1 − 0.75·crown cover
  (as for the sky). The brief gives the SOLWEIG form α_w·G·(1 − ψ) from memory; this is an *interpretation*. Glass walls are
  left out of it (they reflect specularly, block 3). K_wr adds to K↓ and to a person's sides.
- Mean radiant temperature [solweig], simplified six-direction sum: Tmrt = ((S_str + L_str)/(a_p·σ))^¼ − 273.15, a_k = 0.7, a_p = 0.97, f_p = 0.308·cos(β(1 − β²/48402));
  K_lat = 0.5·K_sky + 0.5·α·K↓ + 0.5·K_wr; glass adds a beam r·I_b from the mirrored sun (same altitude, same f_p).
- Pedestrian route cost [melnikov]: sun distance + w_b × building-shade distance + w_t × tree-shade distance, with w_b = 1 − 0.16·s and w_t = 1 − 0.08·s.
  w_b = 0.84 at full heat is cited; w_t = 0.92 is an *interpretation* of "tree shade about half as effective"; the heat factor s rises linearly from 0 at air ≤ 26 °C to 1 at air ≥ 32 °C (*engine assumption*, not in the source). The same weights drive the sideways drift toward shade within a pavement (only when air > 28 °C).

## Block 2: retrofit parameters

The second block (`cities/sponge-retrofit`) shows one invented 400 m block twice: **Before** (dark membrane
roofs, dense asphalt, few trees) and **After** a sponge-city and cool-roof retrofit. Its materials are in the
parameter table above (`coolroofAged`, `coolroofNew`, `greenroof`, `pavers`, `porousAsphalt`,
`coolPaveAged`, `coolPaveNew`, `raingarden`, `youngCanopy`); its research brief is
[`docs/research/retrofit-brief.md`](research/retrofit-brief.md).

- **Aged / new.** The page defaults to aged materials (cool roof α 0.60, coating α 0.18); "new" switches to
  α 0.80 and 0.25. Aged cool-roof albedo 0.60 sits in the aged range 0.50–0.70, of which 0.60–0.70 is unverified.
- **Days since rain d** (0–7, default 3; d = 0 is a clear day after overnight rain). Wetted materials
  decay from wet to dry: X(d) = X_dry + (X_wet − X_dry)·exp(−d/τ), for the evaporative fraction and, for the green
  roof, the substrate admittance μ (600 dry → 1200 wet). τ = 1 d for pavers and porous asphalt (calibrated to
  Li 2013 in the brief), 3 d for rain-garden soil (McColl 2017 drydown; median not checked), 4 d for green-roof
  substrate (unverified). Irrigated lawn does not decay. For example the pavers' evaporative fraction is
  0.50 at d = 0, 0.20 at d = 1 and 0.04 at d = 3. EF(d) is held for the whole day.
- **Young street trees** transmit exp(−0.5·LAI) = 0.47 of the sun with LAI 1.5 (brief range 0.37–0.61 for LAI 1–2,
  unverified); mature trees keep 0.17.
- **Shared run.** Before is run in full. After reuses every Before cell whose inputs (material parameters, sky view
  factor, sunlit share in all 144 sun slots) are identical, and runs the other 2937 cells with the same
  force-restore scheme and convergence test (this run: 7 days, final max |ΔTd| = 0.028 K).
  After is always derived from the same Before run, so a state does not depend on the order the controls were used.
- **Air temperature is a fixed forcing**, identical in both states: the model cannot show air cooling, and the
  page does not claim any. Air-temperature effects on the cards are quoted from the literature and labelled so.

### Who cools the walkers

At 15:00 the share of route sample points (every 2 m along both pavements of every walker route) where a person's
Tmrt is 65 °C or more — the sweat-cue threshold — is 69.9% Before, 66.3% After, and
69.9% for After **without the young street trees** (mean Tmrt 65.0, 64.5 and 65.2 °C).

## Sponge city

The sponge-city card follows the State Council General Office guideline of October 2015 (国办发〔2015〕75号) [sponge2015]:
manage rain where it falls — let it soak in, slow it, store it, clean it, use it, then drain it (渗、滞、蓄、净、用、排) — with
green roofs, permeable paving, rain gardens and wetlands; absorb and use 70% of rainfall on site, applied as a
control rate for total annual runoff (年径流总量控制率); 20% of built-up area by 2020 and 80% by 2030. Flooding and water
supply come first; cooling is a co-benefit. **No robust measured block-scale cooling figure for sponge-city retrofits was
found, so the page quotes none.** The primary Chinese text was not located; the summary follows the English notice
(gov.cn, 2015-10-16), a reprint (waizi.org.cn/law/6860.html) and a MOHURD interpretation (cs.com.cn), so it is marked
unverified on the card. Rain, runoff and flooding are not modelled.

## Trade-offs

- **Reflective (cool) pavement cools the road, not the person.** The model's Tmrt already includes sunshine reflected
  up from the ground, so a lighter street raises a pedestrian's midday Tmrt while its surface cools (Middel et al.
  2020 measured about +4 K Tmrt and −4 to −6 K surface in Los Angeles). The card says so and pairs it with shade.
  Walls now reflect sunshine diffusely (wall albedo 0.3, half the wall taken as sunlit; #8), so light bounced onto
  the pavements from building faces is included in a simple, street-averaged form (Middel saw +20–30 W m⁻² on
  sidewalks in the evening).
- **Porous asphalt is hotter when dry.** It is darker (α 0.08) and lighter-weight than dense asphalt, so it only
  cools in the first day or two after rain (Li et al. 2013: dry porous asphalt about +5 K).
- **Pavers are lighter, so they also reflect more sun onto people** at midday once dry; the pavers card shows ΔTmrt.
- **Ageing:** a coating that starts at 0.26 is about 0.18 after a year (Ko et al. 2022); in-service cool roofs often sit
  below their aged rating (LBNL-6714E). The page defaults to aged values.

### Glass optics

The brief's table is a little higher at 60–80° (0.41 vs 0.385 at 75°): it matches applying the two-surface
formula 2R/(1 + R) to the polarisation-averaged single-surface R. Both are inside the acceptance band at 75° (0.41 ± 0.03).
Coated glass keeps the clear-glass angular shape, scaled so that ρ(0) = ρ₀ and ρ(90°) = 1:
ρ(θ) = ρ₀·(1 − ρ̄(θ)) + ρ̄(θ), ρ̄(θ) = (R(θ) − R(0))/(1 − R(0)). This is the EnergyPlus form for coated glass [energyplus]
(origin given there as Finlayson et al. 1993, WINDOW 4.0 documentation, LBL-33943; not checked), with two differences:
the engine uses exact normalised Fresnel for ρ̄ instead of EnergyPlus's 4th-order polynomial fit in cos θ (largest
difference 0.032 at 84°, the fit is a little lower), and EnergyPlus switches to a bronze-glass curve when the normal
transmittance is ≤ 0.645 (likely for low-e and mirror products), while the engine always uses the clear shape. LBNL finds
coated glass up to about 8% off the clear-glass shape at 60°. **At grazing incidence every glass reflects strongly**: at 15:00 the sun is
at azimuth 261°, so the south faces are lit at about 84° and reflect 0.7–0.8 of the beam whatever the glass type.

### Specular reflection

Single bounce of the direct beam off every sunlit glass façade [brief3]: mirrored sun vector s′ = s − 2(s·n)n (same
altitude, mirrored azimuth); for each ground sample p, if the ray from p along s′ first hits glass of façade f at a point
that is itself in sun (both rays tested against every other building, clipped to the space in front of the façade, and
crowns transmitting as for shade), p receives ρ(θ)·I_b·sinβ on the ground and a person there an extra beam ρ(θ)·I_b
(S_str += a_k·f_p·ρ·I_b). The reflected beam is evaluated where it reaches the ground (z = 0), not at a person's height,
a negligible shift (about 1 m along the beam). Coverage across the façade normal is clipped analytically: a sample stands
for a square, and a strip thinner than a sample (grazing incidence) is counted with its true area, so no façade lights more
ground than its unobstructed area A·cosθ/sinβ (checked in the block, 8:00–17:00). Samples: 2 × 2 per 5 m cell, every 10-min sun slot, only under each sunlit façade's reflected
beam (the projection of the façade along −s′, landing up to H/tanβ away). Reflections land on the ground only (not on
lower roofs). Flat façades do not focus: no point gets more than Σ ρ(θ)·I_b·sinβ over the façades that face it.

### Walkers and the sweat cue

Walkers' route cost treats a reflection spot by its Tmrt: a class sits between building shade (w_b) and sun (1) in
proportion to where its mean Tmrt sits between plain shade and plain sun at that hour, w = w_b + (1 − w_b)(Tmrt − Tmrt_shade)/(Tmrt_sun − Tmrt_shade)
(*engine assumption* built on [melnikov]); spots in sun weigh more than 1. At 15:00 (low-e) the weights are 0.885 for a spot in shade and 1.045
for a spot in sun (building shade 0.840). People per m² of walkable ground at 15:00: plain shade 1.770, spots in shade 1.346,
all spots 0.764, sun 1.420 (per 1000 m²). The sweat cue follows Tmrt (65 → 70 °C) for anyone in sun or in a
reflection spot; shaded spots stay below 65 °C here, so in practice it shows in sunlit spots.

### Glass surface temperature

T_g = Ta + a_g·I_f/(h_o + h_i), I_f = I_b·max(0, cos θ) + 0.5·D per façade orientation (the brief's equation, a sunlit façade taken as fully lit; *assumption*,
no measurement found): at 15:00 on a face lit head-on, Ta + 3.3 K (clear) to Ta + 13.4 K (mirror); ≈ Ta at night. Glass walls emit
ε_g·σT_g⁴ and reflect (1 − ε_g)·σTa⁴; each cell's wall view is split into opaque and glass (per orientation) by the horizon scan:
in each direction the building that sets the horizon takes that direction's wall view through the face the ray enters
(*engine assumption*: lower walls in front of it are not counted separately).

### AC rejected heat (information only)

Rejected heat = Q_cool·(1 + 1/COP) (at COP 3, 100 W of cooling rejects about 133 W). Dense urban areas 20–70 W m⁻² of
ground area, occasionally > 100 [xie2016]; central Tokyo > 400 W m⁻² by day [sailor2011]. No figure per m² of CBD office
floor was found. **The air temperature is fixed, so this heat is not felt in this model**: it is not added to any
surface temperature or Tmrt; the overlay only marks rooftop plant.

- **Wind:** one site wind speed (1.5 m/s) sets convection everywhere; no channelling along canyons, no vortices, no
  tower downdraughts, no sheltering.
- **Air temperature is fixed:** no canyon air heat island, no warming of air in reflection zones (Chengdu: +1–2 °C), no AC
  waste heat.
- **AC heat is shown, not felt** (see above).
- **Second bounce ignored:** light reflected twice (off the opposite glass) carries ρ² of the beam, 0.04–0.16 of the first
  bounce for ρ 0.2–0.4 at normal incidence, more at grazing angles.
- **Concave façades are out of scope:** flat façades cannot focus. 20 Fenchurch Street (2013) focused up to a simulated
  3,384 W m⁻² (worst 4,176) [zhu2018]; the Vdara pool deck (2010) is anecdotal [vdara]; in scale models concave façades
  raised pedestrian sunshine by more than 300% [speroni2022]. The extreme-case card says so.
- Reflections land on ground only; lower roofs next to towers receive none. Glass reflects only specularly here (its
  diffuse part is ignored) and opaque walls only diffusely.
- Low-e and mirror reflectances, all absorptances, glass emissivity and the glass temperature balance are unverified
  (no datasheet or measurement was found).

## Block 4: waterfront and wind corridors

The fourth block (`cities/waterfront`) is an invented 400 m block on the south bank of a river: a stone promenade
with a row of riverside trees, two wide streets running south from the river along the prevailing wind (Quay Street
and Willow Street, the ventilation corridors), two narrower streets across it (Rope Walk and Tannery Lane, in the lee
of the building rows upwind of them) and a garden with a small still pond. The wind blows from the river (north) at a
calm (1 m/s) or breezy (3 m/s, default) preset; buttons, `?u=1|3`. Its research brief is
[`docs/research/waterfront-brief.md`](research/waterfront-brief.md).

### Wind factor

Each 5 m ground cell gets a wind factor f_w = clamp(S·C, 0.3, 1.2) (`engine/wind.js`):

- **Shelter S** = min over upwind buildings b within 5·H_b of f_min + (1 − f_min)·d_b/(5·H_b), 1 if none;
  d_b is the distance from the cell centre, straight upwind, to b's footprint (an exact ray–box distance).
- **Channelling C** = 1 + c_ch·max(0, cos 3δ) in a street within 30° of the wind (δ the angle between its axis
  and the wind) whose H/W > 0.4; else 1. A street here is its carriageway and pavements out to the facing walls;
  H is the mean height of the walls facing it within 15 m of the kerb and W the mean wall-to-wall width, both
  sampled every 5 m where walls face it on both sides.
- **Roofs** keep f_w = 1 (*engine assumption*: above the street-level shelter). Trees are not obstacles (porous; not
  modelled).
- The local wind u_i = u_site·f_w sets the convective coefficient of the cell, h_c = 5.7 + 3.8·u_i [oke], in the
  force-restore model, and the wind a walker feels. **Blocks 1–3 declare no wind direction, have no f_w and keep the
  scalar h_c = 5.7 + 3.8·u_site**: their model runs, drawings and pixels are unchanged (checked against the pages
  built before this block, `tests/e2e/regression.test.mjs`).
- Block 4 runs one model per preset, each with 6 coarse (10-min) spin-up days before the 1-min days (as block 3,
  with the same stability guard); this run converges after 3 1-min days at 3 m/s
  (max |ΔTd| 0.010 K) and 3 at 1 m/s.

### Air overlay (information only)

The air temperature stays a fixed forcing. The water's effect on the air is drawn as an overlay along the wind lines
(`A`, `?air=1`), labelled "illustrative, from literature, not simulated", and never enters a temperature, Tmrt or
comfort value. Along a line, λ = ∫ds/L(s) from where it leaves the water, L = 30 m (60 m in streets along the
wind); by day ΔTa = −1 K·exp(−λ), at night +0.3 K·exp(−λ) (day weight from the sun altitude, full above
10°). The pond uses the river's numbers ×0.20 (−0.2 K by day [jacobs2020]; at night the same ratio,
an *engine assumption*). By day: −1.00 K at the bank, −0.39 K at 30 m, −0.13 K at 80 m.

### Wind lines (drawing only)

Seeds every 20 m across the wind on the upwind edge (seeded jitter ±3 m); RK2 steps of 2.5 m along the wind, or,
where a step would enter a building (grown by 1.5 m), along the wall: the side the wind leans toward, head-on the
side of the wall's centre the line is on. A line ends at the block edge or where it meets an earlier line (on a
1.5 m grid), so no path is drawn twice. Lines are recorded once through the pen recorder (a slow seeded wobble) and
dashed every frame: dash period 4 m per m/s of local wind (∝ u_i), moving at 4·u_i (drawn faster than life);
the phase is a pure function of the frame. Label on the page: "assumed direction × shelter factor, not computed flow".

### Comfort and walkers

Walkers use the block 1 route rule (Melnikov shade weights, scaled by heat) with a wind term
(`engine/waterfront.js` comfortShade): per 2 m pixel, w = w_class + (1 − w_b)·(AT(u·f_w) − AT(u))/(AT_sun − AT_shade),
with AT the apparent temperature with radiation [steadman1994] from the pixel's Tmrt (sun, building shade or tree shade
of its 5 m cell) and the local wind (u_10·f_w clamped to 0.5–17 m/s as the brief gives for UTCI), and AT_sun − AT_shade
the mean gap between sunlit and building-shaded walkable ground at f_w = 1 (8.2 K at 15:00, 3 m/s). At f_w = 1 the
weights are exactly those of blocks 1–3; a breezier pixel weighs less, a sheltered one more. UTCI is not implemented;
apparent temperature is a simpler stand-in (coefficients from memory, unverified). The sweat cue is unchanged (Tmrt only).

At 15:00 (3 m/s) all 14 walkers on the stroll route take the tree-shaded, breezy riverside (cost 373.8) over sunny,
sheltered Rope Walk (423.1). Across all routes the wind term moves walkers only a little: mean f_w where people walk
1.00 vs 0.99 with the wind ignored (walkable ground 0.87). Shade dominates: under the riverside trees
Tmrt is 42.4 °C vs 68.4 °C in the sun beside them (apparent temperature 38.0 vs 46.6 °C), against
the overlay's −1 K at the bank.

### Disclosures (block 4)

- **Air fixed:** the air temperature is the same design-day curve as in blocks 1–3; the water's air effect is an overlay
  only. Measured urban water cools the air only a little and over a short distance (about 0.2–1.5 K by day, mostly
  within 30–80 m [hathway2012, cheonggyecheon, jacobs2020]; reviews that report about 2.5 K are inflated by remote-sensing
  surface temperatures [volker2013]) and can warm the night [steeneveld2014, theeuwes2013, solcerova2019].
- **One wind direction and speed; a geometric heuristic, not CFD:** no vortices, downdraughts, corner jets, river or sea
  breezes, gusts or humidity. Wakes are straight shadows of the footprint along the wind; oblique winds would also
  shelter the corridors' edges. Trees do not slow the wind. Roofs keep f_w = 1.
- **Prescribed water curves** for the river and pond (not a water energy balance).
- **Shade is the biggest comfort lever near water** [jacobs2020: PET −1 to −7 K for shade vs −0.6 K typical next to small
  water bodies]; the riverside-shade card says so.
- **Comfort** uses apparent temperature [steadman1994] (from memory, unverified), not UTCI.
- Unverified: the Hathway & Sharples numbers (secondary sources), the Ota River reach, the Oke (1988) thresholds, the decay
  length 30–80 m, the "< 1 K" river range; f_min, c_ch, f_max, the 5 H recovery, the water curves and the night overlay
  are assumptions.
